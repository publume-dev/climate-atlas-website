Elephant Butte Reservoir, the largest reservoir in New Mexico, dropped to 1.4% capacity on July 27, 2026, its lowest level since 1971.

The critically low water level affects regions relying on the reservoir, particularly farmers and water managers in the Elephant Butte Irrigation District. NASA's Western Water Action Office supports projects that use satellite data to help the district optimize water allocation and groundwater management.

The 2026 low water level was driven by extreme drought in the Rio Grande basin and the earliest snowmelt on record at the river's headwaters.

The article does not provide details on the exact historical low levels or the specific methodology behind the capacity percentage, though it cites the Texas Water Development Board data.
