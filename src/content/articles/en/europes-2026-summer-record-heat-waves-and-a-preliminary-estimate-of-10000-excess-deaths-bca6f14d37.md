A series of at least five heat waves affected Western Europe from May to August 2026, with temperatures exceeding 40°C across a broad area by mid-August. London reached 35.1°C on May 26, breaking the previous May record by 2.3°C. Bordeaux, France, broke its maximum temperature record on three consecutive days in June, reaching 42.5°C on June 24. Combined June and July temperatures in Western Europe were the highest on record, according to Copernicus.

Preliminary reports suggest heat may have been associated with about 10,000 excess deaths across Europe during the 2026 summer heat waves, including thousands in the UK, France, Germany, and Belgium. This figure is not yet confirmed by formal attribution studies. Only 23% of homes in Europe have air conditioning, compared to 90% in the US, contributing to higher heat-related death rates in European cities. The World Health Organization notes that heat stress is the leading cause of weather-related deaths globally, exacerbating underlying conditions such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, mental health issues, and asthma.

A study published in The Lancet Planetary Health in August 2026 projects that increasing heat will lead to intolerable conditions for young, middle-aged, and older adults in coming decades, with risks falling disproportionately on older people.
