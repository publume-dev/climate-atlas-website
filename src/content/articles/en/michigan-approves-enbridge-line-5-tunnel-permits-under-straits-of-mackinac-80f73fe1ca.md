On July 15, 2026, the Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy approved two permits for Enbridge to build a tunnel for the Line 5 pipeline beneath the Straits of Mackinac. The approvals do not guarantee the project will proceed: Enbridge still needs a permit from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and a new permit from the Michigan Public Service Commission.

If fully approved, Enbridge could operate the tunnel and pipeline through the Straits for 99 more years. The Bay Mills Indian Community has been building alternatives to fossil fuels: it has helped members install heat pumps, installed solar panels on tribal buildings, and is developing a $17 million solar farm.

The approvals came in a period marked by broader legal and environmental disputes over Line 5. In late July 2026, the Michigan Supreme Court ruled that the Michigan Public Service Commission failed to fully consider environmental impacts when it approved a tunnel permit in 2023, and vacated that permit. In June 2026, Enbridge spilled drilling fluid into sensitive wetlands in Wisconsin during a Line 5 reroute project.

Reporting on the moment the permits were approved notes that Climate Central's analysis found the scorching temperatures in areas where the wildfires started were made three to five times more likely by climate change. The article does not provide Climate Central's underlying report, leaving that attribution calculation unavailable for independent verification.
