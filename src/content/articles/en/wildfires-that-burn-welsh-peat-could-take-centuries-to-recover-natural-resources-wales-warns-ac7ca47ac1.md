Natural Resources Wales officer Nick Thomas said that if wildfire burns into and incinerates peat soil in upland areas, vegetation could take centuries to recover because peat grows at about one millimetre per year.

Thomas also said that losing soil to wildfires could increase flood risks, because the soil no longer acts as a sponge holding water on high ground.

A wildfire in the Rhinogydd mountains in Gwynedd burned for nearly two weeks and affected more than 2,000 acres within a National Nature Reserve and Special Area of Conservation. Thomas said 2026 appears worse than any wildfire season he can remember and he expected it to be the worst year for wildfires Wales has ever experienced.

Century-scale recovery is conditional on how deeply fire burns into the soil, and no data is given on the proportion of burned area with incinerated soil. The 'worst year' assessment is a mid-season expectation, not final annual statistics. No formal climate-change attribution is provided for the Wales fires; the climate link appears as a volunteer's commentary.
