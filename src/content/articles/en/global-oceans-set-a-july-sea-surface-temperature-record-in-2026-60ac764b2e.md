The Copernicus Climate Change Service said the average sea surface temperature outside polar regions reached 20.96°C in July 2026, beating the previous July record of 20.89°C set in 2023. The same dataset puts July 2026 as the joint second-warmest July globally, at 1.47°C above the estimated pre-industrial average.

Europe saw the effects in both ocean and land records. Sea surface temperatures around Europe set July records along the Atlantic coast and in the western Mediterranean, contributing to widespread strong and severe marine heatwaves. Western Europe's average temperature for June–July was 21.62°C, 2.79°C above the seasonal average and the highest on record for that period. England and Wales recorded their driest July since records began 190 years ago, according to the Met Office.
