According to a study published in Frontiers in Marine Science, sea ice loss in East Greenland has allowed humpback, minke, and fin whales to move into previously ice-blocked coastal waters. Ship-based surveys recorded no humpback sightings before 2006, seven in 2007, and 150 in 2024.

Researchers estimated that about 4,000 humpback, 6,000 fin, and 6,000 minke whales visited the Greenland Sea in summer 2024, based on aerial surveys, satellite tracking, and Inuit hunter reports. This shift is described as a 'major ecological regime shift' in the East Greenland ocean environment, potentially affecting Arctic species such as narwhals and belugas, which may face habitat loss or displacement.

The study does not determine whether the increase in whale sightings represents an overall population increase or a redistribution from other areas, such as West Greenland.
