The Royal Entomological Society is updating conservation guidance for grassland sites in Gloucestershire, Somerset and Wiltshire, funded by Natural England and the Crown Estate, to help prevent extinctions of six rare species amid the UK's hottest summer.

The six species included are the large blue butterfly, Duke of Burgundy butterfly, marsh fritillary butterfly, wart-biter cricket, juniper, and burnt-tip orchid, all found in lowland chalk grassland. The updated guidance will directly affect conservation management for these species under extreme heat.

The project is funded through Natural England's £60 million Species Recovery Programme as part of the Wild Again initiative. This year is the first on record when 35°C has been reached in three different calendar months in the UK, and it was the driest July in England and Wales since records began 190 years ago.
