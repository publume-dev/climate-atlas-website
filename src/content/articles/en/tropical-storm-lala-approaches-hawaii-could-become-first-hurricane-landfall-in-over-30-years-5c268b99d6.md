The National Hurricane Center reported that as of 5 a.m. HST on August 14, 2026, Tropical Storm Lala was located about 420 miles southeast of Hilo with maximum sustained winds of 60 mph, and it was expected to potentially reach hurricane status before nearing the Big Island. A hurricane warning was in effect for the Big Island, with potential rainfall of 8 to 12 inches and locally up to 25 inches, posing a life-threatening flood risk.

Only five named tropical cyclones have made landfall in Hawaii since 1949. If Lala makes landfall, it would be the first since Post-Tropical Cyclone Linda in 2021 and the first hurricane landfall since Hurricane Iniki in 1992.

Sea surface temperatures along Lala's track were about 0.5°C above average for mid-August. Climate Central's Climate Shift Index indicated that these temperatures were made 10 to 20 times more likely by climate change.

The exact track of Lala remains uncertain. The official forecast keeps the center up to 50 miles south of the Big Island, but an onshore track is possible within the cone of uncertainty.
