Three of France's 57 nuclear reactors shut down and seven more ran at reduced output during the July 2026 heatwave, pushing nuclear production down by almost 9%. High temperatures also cut wind power: in the UK in June 2026 wind generation fell to around 15% of the electricity mix from an average of about 30%. At 40C, a gas-fired power station can generate roughly 13% less capacity and run about 7% less efficiently than at 20C, according to Electric Insights.

Plants with once-through river cooling are most exposed: around 60 of the world's 440 nuclear reactors use this system, including eight in France. On 3 August 2026, about 2.44GW, or 40%, of south-east Europe's nuclear capacity was offline because of drought in the Danube, according to data company Montel. Gas capacity also dropped during California's August 2020 heatwave blackouts, when gas plants made up around 79% of the capacity that fell off the system on 14 August. Across Australia, northern Asia and Europe, wind output decreased by an average of 30–50% during heatwaves over 1980–2023, though some regions saw slight increases.

Historical experience puts recent losses in proportion: between 2003 and 2022, heatwaves cut annual nuclear generation by an average of 0.6%, and France's 2003 loss of 1.3% was the only time a national fleet lost more than 1% in a year. Adaptation appears to have reduced the damage. France's river-cooled nuclear fleet lost 5.5TWh to the 2003 heatwave but only 0.5TWh in 2022, a roughly 90% reduction, after utilities upgraded cooling systems, refined operations and scheduled maintenance around heat extremes. Reactor shutdowns due to high river temperatures are typically automatic responses to regulations protecting local ecosystems rather than technological faults, and the regulations are sometimes waived when electricity is needed most.
