According to BBC News’ report on the documentary, whistleblower Douglas Gowan identified 12 locations in Wales where Monsanto allegedly dumped PCBs and other industrial chemicals. The report also says Wrexham resident Michael Phillips, who played at a contaminated lagoon as a child, had blood tests showing higher-than-average PCB levels, and that Sheen’s own blood test found 81 different types of PCBs despite his not living near dump sites.

Wrexham council has said investigations concluded contaminants are present at Llwyneinion but that the site does not meet the legal threshold requiring remediation. For residents who used the lagoon and others focused on former industrial sites, that means contamination can be detected without mandatory cleanup being triggered.

The BBC report sets the allegations in a wider Welsh context: a 2015 Natural Resources Wales survey identified more than 9,000 potential sites requiring further investigation; the Welsh government says it intends to develop a Wales-wide strategy to progressively restore post-industrial land; and Friends of the Earth Cymru is calling for a public inquiry into toxic sites because climate change-related flooding could spread contaminants.

The report does not establish a causal link between PCB exposure and the reported cancers, presenting the dumping claims as allegations and individual cases. Eastman Chemical Company, which is responsible for Monsanto’s legacy production in the UK, did not respond to requests for comment.
