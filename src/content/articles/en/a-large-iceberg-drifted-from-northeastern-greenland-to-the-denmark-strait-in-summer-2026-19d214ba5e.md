In summer 2026, a large iceberg drifted more than 1,000 kilometers south from a bay in northeastern Greenland to the Denmark Strait, where it was imaged by Landsat 9 on June 12. The iceberg measured roughly 17 square kilometers, about five times the area of New York City's Central Park, which is large by Greenland standards.

Keld Quistgaard of the Danish Meteorological Institute indicated the iceberg originated in Jøkelbugten, a bay in northeastern Greenland, possibly from Zachariæ Isstrøm or its adjacent remnant ice shelf. As of mid-August 2026, the iceberg was about 1,500 kilometers from the bay, drifting south on the Greenland Coastal Current, and satellite imagery suggested it was disintegrating. This event offers an opportunity to study iceberg calving and drift, which is relevant for understanding glacier response to climate change.

The precise origin of the iceberg within Jøkelbugten is uncertain; experts differ on whether it calved from Zachariæ Isstrøm or the remnant ice shelf.
