The Global Carbon Budget 2025 report shows that global land-use emissions have fallen by approximately one-third this century compared to the 2000s average. The report notes a statistically significant decrease in land-use emissions since the late 1990s, with a steep drop after 2015.

The decline is driven by decreasing deforestation emissions in Brazil and Indonesia, combined with increased afforestation and reforestation in China. Despite the global positive trend, Brazil, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Indonesia contributed more than half of global land-use emissions over 2015-24, with Brazil and Indonesia showing declining trends.
