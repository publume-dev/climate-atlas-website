In July 2026, Puerto Rico's Governor Jenniffer González declared a state of emergency as key dam levels dropped, leading to rotating water rationing of 48 hours or more for over 500,000 people in seven municipalities. The U.S. Drought Monitor indicates that 72% of the island's land area is experiencing drought, compounded by high temperatures, sedimentation, deteriorating infrastructure, and mismanagement.

Approximately 65% of potable water in Puerto Rico is lost to leaks or unbilled usage, directly affecting water security for residents. Historical efforts such as cloud seeding in 2015 showed no verifiable benefits and no significant contribution to reservoir recovery, suggesting that technological fixes may not suffice without addressing systemic issues.

The emergency highlights challenges in water resource management under climate change. The U.S. Drought Monitor data shows severe drought across much of Puerto Rico, while underinvestment in infrastructure and unequal resource distribution further weaken response capacity.
