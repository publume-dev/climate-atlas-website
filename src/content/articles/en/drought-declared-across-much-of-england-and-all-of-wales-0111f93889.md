Drought has been declared across large parts of central, eastern and southern England and the whole of Wales, according to environmental bodies. England and Wales both provisionally recorded their driest July on record.

Nine UK water companies have announced restrictions, affecting about 25 million people. The Environment Agency says more than half of England is in drought, while Natural Resources Wales says the whole of Wales is in drought; almost all river flows in England are classed as below normal and England's reservoirs are below average for the time of year.

The Met Office expects the UK to experience drier summers on average as the world warms, though there has been no clear trend so far. The UK government and water companies are planning nine new reservoirs for England by 2050, in addition to one under construction at Havant Thicket.

The 'driest July on record' figures for England and Wales are provisional and may be revised after final quality checks. The article does not attribute this specific drought to climate change; the Met Office projection describes a long-term average, and the article notes there has been no clear summer rainfall trend so far.
