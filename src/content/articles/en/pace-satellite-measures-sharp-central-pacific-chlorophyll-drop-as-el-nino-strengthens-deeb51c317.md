NASA reports that during a strengthening El Niño, observations from its PACE mission showed substantially lower chlorophyll-a concentrations in the central Pacific in June 2026 than in June 2025, when conditions were neutral.

During an El Niño, weakened easterly trade winds and a deeper warm surface layer suppress the upwelling of nutrient-rich water that fuels phytoplankton, according to the report. That reduces food for zooplankton, fish, seabirds, and marine mammals. In 2026, Peru's Ministry of Production repeatedly suspended anchovy fishing to protect the country's main fishing resource, and pelicans have been seen venturing into Peruvian ports and urban areas in search of food.

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center expects the El Niño to keep strengthening through the end of 2026, with a 97 percent chance it will last through early Northern Hemisphere spring 2027. Because PACE launched in February 2024, this is the first complete El Niño event for which the satellite will gather global, near-daily hyperspectral measurements.

The NASA report's June 2025–June 2026 comparison is qualitative; it does not provide quantitative chlorophyll concentrations or statistical uncertainty bounds.
