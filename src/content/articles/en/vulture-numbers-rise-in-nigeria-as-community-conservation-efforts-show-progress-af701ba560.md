In Awka-Etiti, Nigeria, volunteers have recorded more than 160 vultures in the area, up from about 20 in 2017. This increase is seen as a result of community-based conservation efforts.

The rise in vulture numbers has direct implications for local ecosystems and communities, as vultures act as scavengers and help prevent disease spread. However, illegal trade remains a threat: Nigeria Conservation Foundation biologist Stella Egbe reports that the price of dead vultures has risen from about 25,000 to 30,000 naira to as much as 200,000 naira per bird.

These conservation efforts in the region come amid a severe decline in African vulture populations. A recent BirdLife report indicates that African vulture populations have fallen by between 80% and 97% over a 50-year period.

The article does not detail the specific methodology of the local volunteer counts, so the reliability of the vulture estimate may be uncertain.
