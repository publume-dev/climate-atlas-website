China’s Ministry of Ecology and Environment (MEE) and 18 other government departments published the national climate-change response plan for 2026–2030 in late July. MEE officials said the document is the first at five-year-plan level to create a comprehensive target system spanning CO2, non-CO2 greenhouse gases, carbon markets, adaptation and international cooperation.

The plan’s headline target is a 17% reduction in carbon intensity over 2026–2030, alongside a 3% cut in carbon intensity per product for industries covered by the carbon market. It also reaffirms building 30 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent of non-CO2 greenhouse gas reduction capacity by 2030. By 2030, China says it aims to markedly increase its influence, guiding power, shaping power and moral appeal in global climate governance and expand the global influence of its carbon market.

To put the non-CO2 goal in context, iGDP’s analysis of China’s UNFCCC reports, cited in the article, estimates China’s total greenhouse gas emissions in 2021 at about 14,000 MtCO2e excluding land-use change and forestry. Non-CO2 gases accounted for around 2,700 MtCO2e, or 19%, primarily methane.

The article does not specify the baseline for the 30 MtCO2e non-CO2 reduction capacity target, so it is unclear against what starting point that capacity is measured.
