July 2026 averaged 76.89°F (24.94°C) across the contiguous U.S., 0.12°F (0.07°C) hotter than July 1936, according to NOAA data reported by Yale Climate Connections. That makes it the warmest month in NOAA records dating to January 1895, surpassing the previous Dust Bowl-era peak.

The warmth shows up across time scales, not only in July. NOAA's Climate at a Glance shows record-high average temperatures for the 6-, 12-, 18-, 24-, 36-, 48-, and 60-month periods ending July 2026, and ranks January–July 2026 as the second-hottest such period on record. Drought accompanied the heat: the Palmer Drought Severity Index put July 2026 as the second-worst July on record for the contiguous U.S., behind July 1934, with six states having their most severe July drought on record and 13 more in the top 10. By August 9, more than 6.3 million acres had burned in U.S. wildfires, already exceeding the area burned in all of 2025. NOAA/NCEI also reports at least 184 all-time high temperatures set or tied at U.S. weather stations in July 2026, bringing the Jan. 1–Aug. 9 total to 213, compared with 20 all-time lows.

These figures are as reported by Yale Climate Connections, based on datasets from NOAA/NCEI, the National Interagency Fire Center, and Gallagher Re. The underlying datasets were not directly reviewed here, so the comparisons to historical records rest on that reporting.
