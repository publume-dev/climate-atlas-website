Hurricane Lala came within 30 miles of the Big Island of Hawaii as a Category 1 hurricane, the closest any hurricane has approached the island on record. Preliminary reports indicate Lala produced at least 43.54 inches of rain at Laupahoehoe, potentially ranking among the top 10 wettest U.S. tropical cyclones and possibly the third-wettest for Hawaii. A peak wind gust of 140 mph was reported at the summit of Mauna Kea.

The storm's close passage and extreme rainfall and winds likely caused localized damage and flooding on the Big Island, though the full extent of impacts is still being assessed. The record closeness and intensity of Lala highlight the potential for significant hurricane impacts in Hawaii under current climate conditions.

Separately, the ongoing El Niño event was just 0.1°C away from the strong category on August 13, and the NWS Climate Prediction Center gives a 69% chance of a historic event exceeding previous El Niño events since 1950.

Rainfall and wind reports are preliminary and may be revised; some estimates are based on radar and may not be final.
