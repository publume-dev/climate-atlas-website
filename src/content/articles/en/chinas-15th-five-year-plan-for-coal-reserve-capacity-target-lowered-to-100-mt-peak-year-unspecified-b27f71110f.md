China's 15th Five-Year Plan for the development of the coal industry was published by the National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration on 10 August 2026, covering 2026-2030. The plan aims to peak coal consumption within the plan period but does not specify a government-endorsed year for the peak.

The plan sets a coal reserve-production capacity goal of 100 million metric tonnes per year by 2030, lower than the previous 300 million tonne target announced in 2024. It also specifies that coal-supply security bases in Shanxi, Inner Mongolia, Shaanxi, and Xinjiang will supply more than 80% of China's coal by 2030.

The plan mandates that new or expanded coal mines in most of these provinces must have a minimum annual production capacity of 1.2 million tonnes, and all new production capacity must be approved by the central government. Additionally, the plan targets 26 billion cubic metres of coalbed-methane production and 6.5 billion cubic metres of mine-gas utilisation by 2030.
